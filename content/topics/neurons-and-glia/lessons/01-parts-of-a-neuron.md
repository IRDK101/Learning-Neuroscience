:::added
**New to this?** A neuron is a cell specialized to send signals. Most are tiny, about 4–20 micrometers across, roughly 25 times narrower than a human hair, yet some axons are long enough to reach from your spine to your toes.
:::

## Inputs: dendrites {#dendrites}

::diagram{id="neuron" highlight="dendrite,dendritic-spine" caption="Signals flow left to right."}

The neuron's **processes** are the branches coming off it. **Dendrites** are the spiky branches where a neuron *receives* messages from other neurons. Little **dendritic spines** line them, each holding a single synapse.

## The cell body {#soma}

::focus{highlight="soma,cell-nucleus"}

The **soma**, or cell body, holds the **nucleus** (the DNA) and the cell's other machinery. The Nissl stain colors cell bodies; the Golgi stain colors the whole neuron, branches and all.

## Output: the axon {#axon}

::focus{highlight="axon-hillock,axon"}

A neuron has many dendrites but only **one axon**, its long output cable. It starts at the **axon hillock**, right next to the cell body, which is where action potentials are triggered.

::focus{highlight="myelin-sheath,node-of-ranvier,oligodendrocyte"}

Many axons are wrapped in **myelin**, a fatty insulating coat like the rubber on an electrical wire. In the CNS, **oligodendrocytes** make it, and each one can wrap several axons. The small gaps between myelin segments, the **nodes of Ranvier**, leave the axon exposed so it can exchange ions with the fluid outside.

::check{q="q-neur-hillock"}

## Branches and endings {#terminals}

::focus{highlight="axon-collateral"}

The single axon can split later into **axon collaterals**, which "CC" the same message to other cells: for example, to a muscle and back to the brain, so the brain can compare what happened with what was supposed to happen.

::focus{highlight="axon-terminal"}

At its end the axon branches into **axon terminals**. Each one forms a **synapse**, usually on a dendrite, sometimes on a cell body or another axon. The terminal doesn't actually touch the next cell; it releases neurotransmitter across a tiny gap.

::check{q="q-neur-one-axon"}
