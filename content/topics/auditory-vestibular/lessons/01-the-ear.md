## What sound is {#sound}

[[Sound|sound]] is a pressure wave in air: bands of compressed air alternating with rarefied (spread-out) air. Two properties matter, and they vary independently:

- **[[Frequency|frequency]]**, the number of cycles per second (Hz), is heard as **pitch**. Higher frequency means higher pitch. The time one cycle takes is the period.
- **[[Intensity|intensity]]** (amplitude), the size of the pressure change, is heard as **loudness**.

Most real sounds are many waves stacked on top of each other.

## Outer ear {#outer}

::diagram{id="ear" highlight="pinna,auditory-canal" caption="The right ear, seen from the front."}

The [[pinna]] is the funnel you can see. It gathers sound into the [[auditory canal|auditory-canal]], which is still part of the outer ear and ends at the eardrum.

## Middle ear {#middle}

::focus{highlight="tympanic-membrane,malleus,incus,stapes"}

Sound makes the [[tympanic membrane|tympanic-membrane]] (eardrum) vibrate. Three tiny bones, the [[ossicles]], pass the vibration along: the [[malleus]] (attached to the drum) knocks the [[incus]], which moves the [[stapes]]. The stapes pushes on the [[oval window|oval-window]] of the cochlea.

So the signal changes form twice: **air wave → mechanical motion → fluid wave**.

::check{q="q-aud-order"}

::focus{highlight="eustachian-tube"}

The [[Eustachian tube|eustachian-tube]] links the middle ear to the back of the nose. When the air pressure around you changes, as on a plane, swallowing opens the tube and lets the pressures match, which is why your ears pop.

Two tiny [[middle-ear muscles|middle-ear-muscles]], the tensor tympani and stapedius, tighten during very loud sounds (and when you talk) to damp the ossicles and protect the inner ear. The whole system is so delicate that it falls apart when dissected, which makes it hard to study.

## Inner ear {#inner}

::focus{highlight="cochlea,semicircular-canals,otolith-organs"}

The inner ear holds two fluid-filled organs: the snail-shaped [[cochlea]] for hearing and the [[vestibular labyrinth|vestibular-labyrinth]] for balance. Their signals leave together in the [[auditory-vestibular nerve|auditory-nerve]] (cranial nerve VIII).
