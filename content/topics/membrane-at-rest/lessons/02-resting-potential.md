## Two forces on every ion {#gradients}

- **Concentration gradient:** particles spread from where they're crowded to where they're sparse, if they can get through.
- **Electrical gradient:** a voltage difference pulls positive ions toward the negative side and negative ions toward the positive side.

Together they form the **electrochemical gradient**. When the two forces push in opposite directions, net movement is small. When they push the same way and a channel opens, ions rush through.

## Inside is negative {#resting}

::diagram{id="membrane" highlight="potassium-leak-channel,potassium-ion"}

At rest, the inside of every neuron is negative relative to the outside, about **–65 mV**. This is the **resting membrane potential**. Remember it; everything else builds on it.

Here's where each ion stands:

::focus{highlight="potassium-ion,potassium-leak-channel"}

- **K⁺** is the only major ion more concentrated *inside*. Diffusion pushes it out, but the negative inside pulls it back in. Because the resting membrane has many open K⁺ leak channels, **K⁺ trickling out is what makes the inside negative** {{C-014}}.

::focus{highlight="sodium-ion"}

- **Na⁺** is much more concentrated *outside*. Both forces push it in, so if a channel opens it floods in quickly. That's the basis of the action potential.

::focus{highlight="calcium-ion,calcium-pump"}

- **Ca²⁺** is even more concentrated outside, about 10,000 to 1. Both forces push it in, but few channels allow it through. Inside the cell it's a powerful trigger ("second messenger"), and a **calcium pump** pushes it back out afterward.

::focus{highlight="chloride-ion"}

- **Cl⁻** is more concentrated *outside*, and its two gradients roughly balance at rest.

::check{q="q-mem-resting"}

## What the pump is for {#pump}

::focus{highlight="sodium-potassium-pump"}

The **sodium-potassium pump** keeps sodium out and potassium in by pushing 3 Na⁺ out for every 2 K⁺ it brings in. It's often described as "creating" the resting potential, but its main job is maintaining the *concentration gradients* that the leak channels rely on. Its direct effect on the voltage is only a few millivolts. If the pumps stopped, the gradients would slowly run down and the cell would drift toward no charge. Pumping costs a lot: often estimated at about half the brain's energy {{F-15}}.

::check{q="q-mem-pump"}
