## Visual fields {#fields}

::diagram{id="visual-pathway" highlight="eye-globe" caption="Blue carries the left visual field, orange the right."}

Everything you can see at once is your [[visual field|visual-field]]. A line straight down the middle splits it into a left and a right **hemifield**. Both eyes see most of the field (the binocular zone), but the far left and far right edges are each seen by one eye only.

The goal of the wiring is simple: the **right hemisphere sees the left hemifield, and the left hemisphere sees the right**, using information from both eyes.

## Partial crossing at the chiasm {#chiasm}

::focus{highlight="optic-nerve,optic-chiasm,optic-tract"}

::pathway{id="retinofugal-projection"}

Ganglion-cell axons leave each eye in the [[optic nerve|optic-nerve]] and meet at the [[optic chiasm|optic-chiasm]]. Only the axons from the **nasal** (inner) half of each retina cross. The nasal retina sees the outer (temporal) part of the visual field, so crossing these axons, and only these, sends each hemifield to the opposite hemisphere ([[partial decussation|partial-decussation]]). After the chiasm, the axons continue as the [[optic tract|optic-tract]].

::check{q="q-vis-cross"}

## What lesions do {#lesions}

Where the damage is decides what's lost:

- **One optic nerve:** that eye goes blind ([[optic nerve damage|monocular-blindness]]), but the other eye still covers almost the whole field.
- **The optic chiasm:** the crossing fibers are cut, so both outer halves of the field disappear. This is [[bitemporal hemianopia|bitemporal-hemianopia]], often caused by a pituitary tumor pressing up on the chiasm.
- **Anywhere after the chiasm** (optic tract, LGN, optic radiation or V1): the whole opposite hemifield is lost in both eyes, a [[homonymous hemianopia|homonymous-hemianopia]].
- **A small patch of V1:** a small blind spot, a [[scotoma]], which the brain often fills in.

::check{q="q-vis-chiasm"}

## The LGN {#lgn}

::focus{highlight="lateral-geniculate-nucleus,optic-radiation,primary-visual-cortex"}

The optic tract ends in the [[lateral geniculate nucleus|lateral-geniculate-nucleus]] (LGN) of the thalamus. The LGN has **six layers**: three take input from the eye on the same side (ipsilateral) and three from the opposite eye (contralateral). Keeping the eyes apart may help with depth perception. From the LGN, the [[optic radiation|optic-radiation]] fans back to V1. This route carries most of what you consciously see.

## The superior colliculus route {#tectum}

::focus{highlight="superior-colliculus"}

About 10% of retinal axons go instead to the [[superior colliculus|superior-colliculus]] in the midbrain ([[retinotectal projection|retinotectal-projection]]). It turns your eyes and head toward things that appear, driving quick eye movements (saccades) and tiny ones (microsaccades). It sends on to visual cortex through the pulvinar of the thalamus. Pupil size is controlled by a different small target, the [[pretectum]].

::check{q="q-vis-sc"}

## V1 {#v1}

The [[primary visual cortex|primary-visual-cortex]] (V1, area 17, striate cortex) lies at the back of the occipital lobe along the calcarine fissure. Each V1 keeps a [[retinotopic map|retinotopy]] of the opposite hemifield. The map is upside down and flipped, so a face in the middle of your view is split between the two V1s, each half upside down. The two eyes' inputs stay separate here too.

[[Simple cells|simple-cell]] in V1 fire best to a bar or edge at one particular angle. They get this tuning from several LGN cells whose receptive fields line up in a row: light along the line excites the cell and light beside it inhibits it.
