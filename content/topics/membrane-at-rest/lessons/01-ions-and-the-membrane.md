## Why this matters {#why}

Neurons talk mostly with **action potentials**: tiny electrical events lasting about 2 ms. They're possible because the neuron's membrane is *excitable*. It can control which ions flow in and out, and when. This topic is about the neuron at rest, the starting state before anything fires.

## Ions in water {#ions}

Both inside and outside the cell, most of the fluid is water. Water is polar, so it dissolves salts into **ions**, atoms with a net positive or negative charge. Four matter most for neurons:

::diagram{id="membrane" highlight="sodium-ion,potassium-ion,chloride-ion,calcium-ion" caption="Outside the cell on top, inside below."}

- **Sodium (Na⁺)**
- **Potassium (K⁺)**
- **Calcium (Ca²⁺)**
- **Chloride (Cl⁻)**

Their different concentrations inside and outside the cell are what make action potentials possible.

## The membrane {#bilayer}

::focus{highlight="phospholipid-bilayer"}

The cell membrane is a **phospholipid bilayer**: two layers of fat molecules. Each has a water-loving head, which faces the water inside or outside, and a water-fearing tail, which hides in the middle. Ions can't cross this oily middle on their own. They need proteins.

## Channels and pumps {#channels}

::focus{highlight="potassium-leak-channel,voltage-gated-sodium-channel,ionotropic-receptor"}

**Ion channels** are pores in the membrane. They don't need energy: ions flow through them down their gradients.

- Some are always open (leak channels, or pores).
- **Ligand-gated** channels open when a chemical binds.
- **Voltage-gated** channels open at a certain voltage. These are critical to action potentials.
- **Mechanically-gated** channels are pulled open by physical force, as when your skin is touched.

::focus{highlight="sodium-potassium-pump,calcium-pump"}

**Pumps** use energy (ATP) to move ions *against* their gradients. The most important is the **sodium-potassium pump**, which pushes 3 Na⁺ out and brings 2 K⁺ in for every ATP it uses.
