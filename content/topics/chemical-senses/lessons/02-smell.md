## The olfactory epithelium {#epithelium}

::diagram{id="olfaction" highlight="olfactory-epithelium" caption="Same color = same receptor type."}

Air reaches the top of the nasal cavity through the nose or from the back of the throat. There, a small patch called the [[olfactory-epithelium]] holds the **olfactory receptor cells**. It's the only place in the body that detects odorants.

::focus{highlight="olfactory-cilia"}

Each receptor cell sends hair-like [[olfactory-cilia]] (its dendrites) into a layer of mucus. The mucus traps odorant molecules so they can reach the receptors on the cilia.

Dogs smell around 100,000 times better than we do. Their epithelium is far larger, and they have more receptor types.

## How a receptor cell responds {#transduction}

::focus{highlight="olfactory-receptor-cell"}

The [[odorant receptors|odorant-receptor]] are G-protein-coupled. Their G protein is called [[g-olf]] (G-olf), so you'll sometimes hear "Golf".

::pathway{id="olfactory-transduction"}

G_olf switches on [[adenylyl-cyclase]], which turns ATP into [[camp]]. cAMP opens channels from the inside that let Na⁺ and Ca²⁺ in. Calcium then opens Cl⁻ channels. Unusually, these cells hold a lot of chloride inside, so Cl⁻ **flows out**, and losing negative charge depolarizes the cell further.

::check{q="q-smell-cl"}

## One receptor type, one glomerulus {#glomeruli}

Humans have about 400 receptor types, and each receptor cell makes just one of them. That doesn't mean we smell only 400 smells. Each receptor responds to many odorants, and each odorant activates several receptors. A smell is the **pattern** across receptors ([[smell-population-code]]).

::focus{highlight="glomerulus,olfactory-receptor-cell"}

All the receptor cells of one type, wherever they sit in the epithelium, send their axons through the [[cribriform-plate]] to the **same** [[glomerulus]] in the [[olfactory-bulb]]. The receptor "label" is kept all the way up.

::focus{highlight="mitral-cell"}

[[Mitral cells|mitral-cell]] pick up from each glomerulus and carry the signal out of the bulb.

::check{q="q-smell-400"}

## Two routes to the cortex {#routes}

::pathway{id="olfactory-pathway"}

From the bulb, smell takes two routes:

1. Through the [[olfactory-tubercle]] to the thalamus and on to the [[orbitofrontal-cortex]].
2. **Directly** to the [[olfactory-cortex]] and the [[amygdala]] in the temporal lobe, with a step via the entorhinal cortex into the [[hippocampus]].

Smell is the only sense that can reach the cortex without passing through the thalamus first. Its direct line to emotion and memory areas may be why a smell can bring back a memory so vividly.

::check{q="q-smell-thalamus"}
