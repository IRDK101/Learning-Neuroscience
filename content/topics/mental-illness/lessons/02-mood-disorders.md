## Major depression {#depression}

[[Mood disorders|affective-disorders]] are disorders of mood (affect). The most common serious one is [[major depression|major-depression]]: prolonged sadness that doesn't match the situation, and a loss of pleasure and motivation, even for things you used to enjoy.

Its symptoms vary widely. Eating too much and eating too little, sleeping too much and sleeping too little, can all be signs. Without good biological markers, "depression" is probably several different disorders that look alike, which may be why treatments that work for one person fail for another.

## Bipolar disorder {#bipolar}

[[Bipolar disorder|bipolar-disorder]] swings between distinct episodes of depression and [[mania]]. Mania is "too much of a good thing": grandiosity, feeling invincible, needing little sleep and taking big risks.

## The monoamine hypothesis {#monoamines}

::diagram{id="synapse" highlight="monoamine-oxidase,reuptake-transporter" caption="Antidepressants act at the monoamine synapse."}

The [[monoamine hypothesis|monoamine-hypothesis]] links depression to too little norepinephrine and serotonin signaling. Drugs that raise them can help:

- **[[MAO inhibitors|mao-inhibitors]]** block monoamine oxidase, the enzyme that breaks down norepinephrine and serotonin in the terminal.
- **[[SSRIs|ssris]]** block serotonin reuptake.

Like SSRIs in anxiety, both take weeks to work, so their benefit seems to come from slower downstream changes rather than from the immediate rise in transmitter.

Predicting who will respond to which treatment is still hard, so people often try several drugs and doses. For bipolar disorder, [[lithium]] taken long-term helps prevent both manic and depressive episodes, though it can have serious side effects.

## Deep brain stimulation {#dbs}

For depression that resists other treatments, electrodes can be implanted to stimulate a specific target. Brain scans help pick the most disrupted circuit. One target is the [[subgenual cingulate cortex|subgenual-cingulate]] (area 25), just below the front of the corpus callosum.
