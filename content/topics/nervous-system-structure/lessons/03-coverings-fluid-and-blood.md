## The meninges {#meninges}

::diagram{id="meninges" highlight="dura-mater,arachnoid-membrane,pia-mater" caption="From the skull down to the brain."}

Between the skull and the brain are three protective membranes, the **meninges**:

::focus{highlight="dura-mater"}

1. The **dura mater** is the tough outer layer, right under the skull.

::focus{highlight="arachnoid-membrane,subarachnoid-space"}

2. The **arachnoid membrane** is a structural, web-like layer that helps absorb impacts. Below it is the **subarachnoid space**, filled with cushioning **cerebrospinal fluid (CSF)**.

::focus{highlight="pia-mater"}

3. The **pia mater** is a delicate layer that hugs the brain's surface, dipping into every groove. (The blood–brain barrier is formed by the walls of the brain's capillaries, not by the pia {{C-008}}.)

**Meningitis** is inflammation of these layers. With no room to swell, they press on the brain.

::check{q="q-struct-meninges"}

## The ventricles {#ventricles}

::diagram{id="brain-coronal" highlight="lateral-ventricles,third-ventricle" caption="Ventricles in a coronal slice."}

Deep in the brain are four fluid-filled cavities, the **ventricles**, which hold CSF. The **left and right lateral ventricles** mirror each other, one in each hemisphere. The **third ventricle** sits on the midline between the two halves of the thalamus, and the **fourth ventricle** is the lowest, between the brainstem and cerebellum.

## Blood supply {#blood}

::diagram{id="brain-arteries" highlight="middle-cerebral-artery" caption="The cortex supplied by each artery, seen from the side."}

Blood is toxic to neurons, so the brain has to be fed without blood touching it directly. Blood comes up the neck mainly through the **internal carotid arteries** and enters the **Circle of Willis**, a ring under the brain. If one part of the ring is blocked, blood can take another route. From there, three pairs of arteries branch out:

::focus{highlight="middle-cerebral-artery"}

- The **middle cerebral artery (MCA)** supplies most of the side of the cortex. A stroke here can cut off major areas, including the language areas, which is why MCA strokes often cause aphasias.

::focus{highlight="anterior-cerebral-artery"}

- The **anterior cerebral artery** goes to the front and inner (medial) surface.

::focus{highlight="posterior-cerebral-artery"}

- The **posterior cerebral artery** goes to the back, including the visual cortex.
