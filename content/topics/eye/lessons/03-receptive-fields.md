## Same signal, opposite responses {#on-off}

Every cone feeds two kinds of bipolar cell at once. Both receive the same glutamate, but they have different receptors.

- **[[ON-center bipolar cells|on-center-bipolar]]** have metabotropic glutamate receptors that are **inhibitory**. Light → less glutamate → less inhibition → the cell depolarizes and excites its ganglion cell.
- **[[OFF-center bipolar cells|off-center-bipolar]]** have ionotropic glutamate receptors that are **excitatory**. Light → less glutamate → the cell hyperpolarizes and its ganglion cell fires less.

Darkness does the opposite to each.

## The surround {#surround}

::diagram{id="retina" highlight="horizontal-cell" caption="Horizontal cells link neighboring photoreceptors."}

[[Horizontal cells|horizontal-cell]] collect from neighboring cones and act on the central pathway. If the neighbors are in the dark, they release more glutamate, which excites the horizontal cell. The horizontal cell releases GABA, which pushes the central pathway the opposite way. Every cone is the "center" for its own pathway and part of the "surround" for its neighbors.

## Center–surround receptive fields {#fields}

The result is that each ganglion cell has a [[center–surround|center-surround]] receptive field:

| | Light in the center | Light in the surround |
|---|---|---|
| **ON-center** ganglion cell | Fires more | Fires less |
| **OFF-center** ganglion cell | Fires less | Fires more |

The center counts for more than the surround, so lighting the whole field still nudges an ON-center cell up a little. What these cells really report is **contrast**: more or less light than the surroundings. Cells sitting on an edge respond most, so the retina highlights the borders of objects before the signal even leaves the eye. Because brightness is judged relative to the surroundings, the same gray can look lighter or darker depending on what's around it.

::check{q="q-eye-center"}

## Color opponency {#color}

Some ganglion cells compare cone types instead of light levels, in **red–green** and **yellow–blue** pairs ([[color opponency|color-opponency]]). A red-center/green-surround cell fires most with red in the center and green around it, and least with the opposite. When the opposing color is suddenly removed, the cell briefly overshoots and fires a burst.
