## Into the spinal cord {#entry}

::diagram{id="spinal-reflex" highlight="dorsal-root-ganglion,dorsal-root,dorsal-horn" caption="Sensory axons enter at the back of the cord."}

Sensory axons enter the spinal cord through the [[dorsal root|dorsal-root]]; their cell bodies sit just outside in the [[dorsal root ganglion|dorsal-root-ganglion]]. In general the **dorsal** (back) half of the cord is sensory and the **ventral** (front) half is motor. Inside the cord each axon branches: some branches feed local reflexes and others head for the brain.

Each spinal segment's nerve serves one strip of skin, a [[dermatome]]. From top to bottom the segments are cervical, thoracic, lumbar and sacral. The strips are easiest to see on someone on all fours. Damage high in the cord is worse than damage low down, because signals from everything below have to pass through.

## Touch: the dorsal column pathway {#dcml}

::diagram{id="long-tracts" highlight="dorsal-column-nuclei" layers="touch" caption="All three pathways serve the right side of the body."}

::pathway{id="dorsal-column-pathway"}

Fine touch and proprioception travel up the **same side** of the cord in the dorsal columns to the [[dorsal column nuclei|dorsal-column-nuclei]] in the medulla. That's the first synapse. The next axon **crosses the midline** and runs up to the thalamus (VPL), and the third neuron carries the signal to the [[primary somatosensory cortex|primary-somatosensory-cortex]] (S1). Three neurons, one crossing, in the medulla.

The face works the same way but through a cranial nerve: the [[trigeminal pathway|trigeminal-pathway]] crosses in the brainstem and reaches the thalamus without involving the spinal cord.

## Pain and temperature: the spinothalamic pathway {#spinothalamic}

::focus{highlight="spinal-cord" layers="pain"}

::pathway{id="spinothalamic-pathway"}

Pain and temperature fibers synapse as soon as they enter, in the dorsal horn. The second neuron **crosses right away, in the spinal cord**, and climbs the opposite side to the thalamus and S1.

::check{q="q-soma-cross"}

## One-sided cord damage {#brown-sequard}

Because the two pathways cross at different levels, damaging one side of the cord gives a split pattern below the injury. This is [[Brown-Séquard syndrome|brown-sequard-syndrome]]:

- **Same side:** loss of fine touch and proprioception (those fibers haven't crossed yet).
- **Opposite side:** loss of pain and temperature (those fibers crossed below the injury).

The pattern tells doctors which side and which level of the cord is damaged.

::check{q="q-soma-brown"}

## S1 and its body map {#s1}

::diagram{id="brain-lateral" highlight="primary-somatosensory-cortex" caption="S1 lies just behind the central sulcus."}

S1 sits on the gyrus just behind the central sulcus. Damage here removes feeling even though the skin is fine. Areas 3a and 3b receive the input and pass it on to area 1 (texture) and area 2 (size and shape) ([[areas of S1|s1-areas]]).

S1 is a map of the body: neighboring body parts are handled by neighboring cortex ([[somatotopy]]). The map is distorted, drawn as a [[homunculus]], because the amount of cortex follows **receptor density**, not body size. Lips and fingertips get huge areas. The map can change, too: skilled string players have expanded finger areas.

::check{q="q-soma-homunculus"}
