## The workhorses {#workhorses}

Most neurons use amino-acid transmitters most of the time. They are small, abundant and easy to make, so they're synthesized right near the axon terminal and packaged into vesicles.

- **Glutamate** is the brain's main **excitatory** transmitter.
- **GABA** and **glycine** are mostly **inhibitory**.

::check{q="q-nt-excitatory"}

## GABA is glutamate plus one enzyme {#gad}

To make GABA, a neuron takes glutamate and uses one extra enzyme, **GAD** (glutamic acid decarboxylase). A GABA neuron is basically a glutamate neuron that has GAD.

::check{q="q-nt-gad"}

## Clearing amino acids {#clearing}

::diagram{id="synapse" highlight="astrocyte,reuptake-transporter"}

Some glutamate and GABA simply diffuses away, and some goes back into the terminal by reuptake. **Astrocytes** play a big role too, picking up both glutamate and GABA from the synapse very quickly.

## Drugs that boost GABA {#gaba-drugs}

::focus{highlight="ionotropic-receptor"}

The **GABA-A receptor** is an ionotropic receptor that is a **chloride (Cl⁻) channel**. When GABA opens it, Cl⁻ flows in and the cell becomes more negative (hyperpolarized), so it's less likely to fire.

- **Benzodiazepines** (e.g. Xanax) bind a separate site on the GABA-A receptor and make GABA open the channel more effectively. They don't open it on their own {{C-079}}.
- **Alcohol** also enhances GABA-A receptors. This may be one reason alcohol use and anxiety disorders often go together.

::check{q="q-nt-benzo"}

## The four systems at a glance {#summary}

| Transmitter | Made from | Main source | Cleared by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Acetylcholine | Choline + acetyl CoA (ChAT) | Basal forebrain, PMT complex | AChE in the cleft; choline recycled |
| Dopamine | Tyrosine → L-DOPA → DA | Substantia nigra, VTA | DAT reuptake; MAO |
| Norepinephrine | Dopamine (DBH, in vesicles) | Locus coeruleus | Reuptake; MAO |
| Serotonin | Tryptophan | Raphe nuclei | SERT reuptake; MAO |
| Glutamate / GABA | Glutamate; GABA via GAD | Almost everywhere | Reuptake, astrocytes, diffusion |
