## Two families {#families}

::diagram{id="synapse" highlight="ionotropic-receptor,metabotropic-receptor"}

::focus{highlight="ionotropic-receptor"}

**Ionotropic receptors** *are* ion channels, which is why they're also called ligand-gated channels. When transmitter binds, the channel opens and ions flow. They're fast.

::focus{highlight="metabotropic-receptor,g-protein"}

**Metabotropic receptors**, also called G-protein-coupled receptors, don't open anything themselves. They act more like ears: when transmitter binds outside, they switch on **G proteins** inside the cell. G proteins can then:

- open separate **G-protein-gated ion channels** from the inside, the "shortcut" pathway {{C-020}};
- activate enzymes that make **second messengers**, which can do almost anything: change channels, even switch genes on to build new proteins.

Metabotropic effects are slower to start but can last longer and be more powerful.

::check{q="q-syn-metabotropic"}

:::note
This sets up the next topic. Because the receptor decides what happens, the same transmitter can excite one cell and inhibit another.
:::
