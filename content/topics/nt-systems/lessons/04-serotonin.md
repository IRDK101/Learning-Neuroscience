## Making serotonin {#synthesis}

::pathway{id="serotonin-synthesis"}

Serotonin is also called **5-HT** (5-hydroxytryptamine). Its precursor is **tryptophan**, an amino acid the body can't make, so it has to come from the diet. The supply of tryptophan is the **rate-limiting** factor for making serotonin. Tryptophan is also the precursor of **melatonin**, the sleep hormone.

:::added
The two enzymes in between (tryptophan hydroxylase, then the same decarboxylase that turns L-DOPA into dopamine) make the chain easier to remember.
:::

## The raphe nuclei {#raphe}

::diagram{id="nt-overlay" layers="serotonin,deep" highlight="raphe-nuclei"}

Serotonin neurons sit in the **raphe nuclei**, a chain of nuclei running down the midline of the brainstem. Their axons go basically **everywhere**: cortex, thalamus, hypothalamus, cerebellum, spinal cord.

Serotonin is involved in **sleep–wake**, **mood and emotion**, and **learning about pleasure**. It's often called a "pleasure chemical", but it's more complicated than that.

## One transmitter, many receptors {#receptors}

Serotonin has an unusually large number of receptor types, each with subtly different effects. It's the poster child for the rule that **the receptor decides what a transmitter does**.

## Clearing serotonin: SSRIs and MAO inhibitors {#ssris}

::diagram{id="synapse" highlight="reuptake-transporter,monoamine-oxidase"}

Like the catecholamines, serotonin is cleared by **reuptake** through a transporter (SERT) and broken down inside the terminal by **MAO**.

::focus{highlight="reuptake-transporter"}

- **SSRIs** (selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors) block the serotonin transporter, so serotonin stays in the synapse longer {{C-080}}.

::focus{highlight="monoamine-oxidase,mitochondrion"}

- **MAO inhibitors** block the enzyme that breaks serotonin (and NE and dopamine) down.

Both are used for depression and anxiety. Here's the puzzle: reuptake is blocked within hours, but people usually feel better only after **2–3 weeks**. So the benefit probably comes from slower, downstream changes in the receiving cells, not simply "more serotonin".
