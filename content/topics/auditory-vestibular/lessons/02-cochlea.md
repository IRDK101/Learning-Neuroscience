## Three chambers {#chambers}

::diagram{id="cochlea" highlight="scala-vestibuli,scala-media,scala-tympani" caption="One turn of the cochlea, cut across."}

Cut across, the cochlea has three fluid-filled chambers. The [[scala vestibuli|scala-vestibuli]] is on top and the [[scala tympani|scala-tympani]] at the bottom. Between them is the [[scala media|scala-media]], which holds the sense organ.

When the stapes pushes the oval window, a fluid wave runs up the scala vestibuli to the apex. There it passes into the scala tympani through a small opening, the [[helicotrema]], and runs back down to the [[round window|round-window]], which bulges out to release the pressure.

::focus{highlight="reissners-membrane,basilar-membrane"}

[[Reissner's membrane|reissners-membrane]] separates the scala vestibuli from the scala media. The [[basilar membrane|basilar-membrane]] separates the scala media from the scala tympani, and it's the one that matters for pitch.

## A frequency map {#place}

The basilar membrane is **narrow and stiff at the base** (near the oval window) and **wide and floppy at the apex**. High frequencies make the base vibrate most; low frequencies travel further and peak near the apex. Where the membrane moves most tells the brain the pitch. This orderly map of frequency is called [[tonotopy]].

::check{q="q-aud-base"}

## The organ of Corti {#corti}

::focus{highlight="organ-of-corti,tectorial-membrane"}

The [[organ of Corti|organ-of-corti]] sits on the basilar membrane. Above it lies the stiff [[tectorial membrane|tectorial-membrane]]. When the basilar membrane moves up and down, the hair cells on it shift against the tectorial membrane, and their [[stereocilia]] bend.

::focus{highlight="inner-hair-cell,outer-hair-cell"}

There are two kinds of [[hair cells|hair-cell]]:

- **[[Inner hair cells|inner-hair-cell]]**, a single row, are the main sensors. About 95% of auditory nerve fibers carry their signals, and each inner hair cell feeds many fibers.
- **[[Outer hair cells|outer-hair-cell]]**, about three times as many, have stereocilia embedded in the tectorial membrane. They change length as they're stimulated and **amplify** the basilar membrane's motion, sharpening sensitivity and tuning. Many outer hair cells share one fiber.

::check{q="q-aud-ohc"}

## Transduction {#transduction}

::focus{highlight="stereocilia"}

::pathway{id="hair-cell-transduction"}

Tip links join neighboring stereocilia. When the stereocilia bend one way, the links pull **mechanically gated K⁺ channels** open. Unusually, the fluid bathing the stereocilia is rich in K⁺, so potassium flows **in** and depolarizes the cell. Hair cells aren't spiking neurons, so this is a graded change. Voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels open and the cell releases **glutamate** onto the [[spiral ganglion|spiral-ganglion]] cells, which fire action potentials into the auditory nerve.

::check{q="q-aud-k"}

## When hair cells are lost {#implants}

Most permanent hearing loss, from aging or loud noise, comes from losing hair cells. A [[cochlear implant|cochlear-implant]] picks up sound and stimulates the auditory nerve directly, bypassing the missing hair cells.
