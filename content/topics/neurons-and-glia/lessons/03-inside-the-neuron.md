## Organelles {#organelles}

Neurons have the same core machinery as other cells:

- The **nucleus** holds the DNA.
- The **rough endoplasmic reticulum** is studded with ribosomes and makes proteins. That matters a lot in neurons, because every channel and pump in the membrane is a protein.
- The **smooth endoplasmic reticulum** makes lipids for the membrane and helps control which ions float around inside the cell.
- The **Golgi apparatus** packages molecules, including transmitters, into vesicles.
- **Mitochondria** make the ATP that powers it all, and neurons need a lot of energy.

## The cytoskeleton {#cytoskeleton}

A neuron's long, branching shape needs scaffolding:

- **Microtubules** are the biggest fibers. They run along the axon and act as tracks.
- **Neurofilaments** are medium-sized fibers that also run lengthwise along the axon. Their side-arms set the spacing between them, helping determine how wide the axon is {{C-013}}.
- **Microfilaments** are the thinnest. They go first when the cell changes shape to make new connections, and neurofilaments then stabilize the new shape.

In **Alzheimer's disease**, a microtubule-stabilizing protein called **tau** clumps into tangles, and the neuron's structure falls apart.

## Shipping along the axon {#transport}

**Axoplasmic transport** moves cargo, such as vesicles of transmitter, up and down the axon on the microtubule "railroad tracks":

- **Kinesin** walks cargo *down* the axon, toward the terminal.
- **Dynein** brings cargo *back* toward the cell body.
