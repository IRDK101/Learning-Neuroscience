## Three shapes of neuron {#types}

- **Multipolar** neurons have many dendrites and one axon. Most neurons look like this: motor neurons, pyramidal cells, Purkinje cells.
- **Bipolar** neurons have one dendrite and one axon, in one end and out the other. Examples are in the retina and the smell system.
- **Pseudounipolar** neurons have their cell body off to the side of a single long process. The signal runs almost straight from the receiving end to the axon, which gives a quick response. Touch-sensing neurons are built this way.

## Glia: the support cells {#glia}

::diagram{id="neuron" highlight="oligodendrocyte,astrocyte,microglia"}

**Glia** are the brain's second major cell type. They don't signal directly; they keep neurons working. The names differ between the CNS and PNS.

::focus{highlight="oligodendrocyte"}

- **Oligodendrocytes** (CNS) wrap myelin around segments of *several* axons at once.

::focus{highlight="astrocyte"}

- **Astrocytes** (CNS) give the brain its structure, exchange nutrients, clean up discarded molecules and regulate the fluid around neurons.

::focus{highlight="microglia"}

- **Microglia** (CNS) are immune cells that pick up bacteria and kill viruses.
- **Ependymal cells** (CNS) line the ventricles and help produce cerebrospinal fluid.
- **Schwann cells** (PNS) make myelin too, but each covers just one segment of *one* axon.
- **Satellite cells** (PNS) do for peripheral neurons what astrocytes do in the brain.

::check{q="q-neur-schwann"}

## When myelin is attacked {#ms}

::focus{highlight="myelin-sheath"}

In **multiple sclerosis**, the immune system attacks myelin. Without their insulation, damaged nerve fibers struggle to pass signals along.
