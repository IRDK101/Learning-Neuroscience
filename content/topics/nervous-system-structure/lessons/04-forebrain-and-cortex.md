## The big picture {#forebrain}

The **forebrain** is the brain's largest division: the cerebrum plus the diencephalon (thalamus and hypothalamus) {{C-009}}. The **longitudinal fissure**, a deep groove down the middle, separates the left and right hemispheres. The surface is folded into bumps (**gyri**) and grooves (**sulci**).

## Grey and white matter {#grey-white}

::diagram{id="brain-coronal" highlight="cerebral-cortex,white-matter,corpus-callosum"}

**Grey matter** is mostly neuron cell bodies, dendrites and synapses. The cortex is a sheet of it {{C-010}}. **White matter** is mostly myelinated axons: the wiring, whose fatty myelin looks white. Some connections are short, some run the length of the body, and some cross between the hemispheres. The biggest crossing, the **corpus callosum**, is a thick bundle of axons linking left and right.

::check{q="q-struct-grey"}

## The lobes {#lobes}

::diagram{id="brain-lateral" highlight="frontal-lobe,parietal-lobe,temporal-lobe,occipital-lobe"}

The cortex has four lobes on the outside:

- The **frontal lobe** at the front.
- The **parietal lobe** behind it, toward the back.
- The **occipital lobe** at the very back.
- The **temporal lobe** on the side, pressing out toward your ears.

::focus{highlight="central-sulcus,lateral-fissure"}

Two landmarks divide them. The **central sulcus** separates the frontal and parietal lobes. The **lateral (Sylvian) fissure** separates the temporal lobe from the lobes above it.

::focus{highlight="primary-motor-cortex,primary-somatosensory-cortex"}

The **primary motor cortex** lies just in front of the central sulcus (frontal lobe). The **primary somatosensory cortex**, for touch, lies just behind it (parietal lobe).

::check{q="q-struct-central-sulcus"}

## A fifth, hidden lobe {#insula}

::diagram{id="brain-coronal" highlight="insula"}

The **insula** is folded deep inside the lateral fissure, covered by the other lobes. It handles primary taste and the processing of emotion and pain, which are important for physical experience. A rule of thumb: evolutionarily older functions, like basic senses, tend to sit closer to the center of the brain.

## Sensory and association cortex {#association}

**Sensory areas** get direct input from a sense. **Association areas** make connections *between* inputs, for example combining a face you see with a voice you hear. They support language, memory, planning and feeling.

## Deep in the forebrain {#deep}

::focus{highlight="caudate-nucleus,putamen,globus-pallidus"}

The **basal ganglia**, made up of the **caudate nucleus**, the **putamen** and the **globus pallidus**, sit near the center and are disrupted in diseases such as Parkinson's. More on them in the movement unit.

::focus{highlight="amygdala,hippocampus"}

The **limbic system** is a center for emotion, memory and feeling:

- The **amygdala**, deep in the temporal lobe, is the brain's fear and threat center.
- The **hippocampus**, just behind it and shaped like a seahorse, helps form memories.
- The **cingulate cortex** wraps around the corpus callosum and helps process feelings, emotions and memory.

::check{q="q-struct-insula"}
