## Two big divisions {#divisions}

::diagram{id="brain-midsagittal" highlight="spinal-cord,midbrain,pons,medulla" caption="The central nervous system: brain plus spinal cord."}

- The **central nervous system (CNS)** is the brain and spinal cord. It's everything encased in bone, which tells you how important it is to protect.
- The **peripheral nervous system (PNS)** is everything else: the nerves branching out to the senses, muscles and organs.

::check{q="q-struct-cns"}

## Inside the PNS {#pns}

The PNS has two parts:

- The **somatic nervous system** handles what you can feel and control: skin senses and the skeletal muscles you move on purpose.
- The **autonomic nervous system** runs organs and glands automatically, things like digestion and heart rate that you don't think about. It has two opposing branches:
  - The **sympathetic** branch is "fight or flight": a faster heart, dilated pupils, energy mobilized {{C-006}}.
  - The **parasympathetic** branch is "rest and digest": it slows the heart and runs digestion and maintenance.

::check{q="q-struct-sympathetic"}

## Cranial nerves {#cranial}

Twelve pairs of **cranial nerves** leave the brain directly, without passing through the spinal cord, to serve the head, face and neck. You don't need their names for this course. They're technically PNS, though some people group them with the CNS because they're so close to the brain. Damage to one can cost you control of part of the face.

The **optic nerve** is one of them. It carries signals from the retina to the **optic chiasm**, the X-shaped crossing under the brain. Only the axons from the *inner (nasal) half* of each retina cross {{C-007}}. The result is that each hemisphere sees the opposite half of the visual field with both eyes.
