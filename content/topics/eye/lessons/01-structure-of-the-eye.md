## Light {#light}

Eyes detect [[visible light|visible-light]], a narrow band of electromagnetic waves from about 400 nm (violet) to 700 nm (red). When light hits an object it can be **reflected**, **absorbed** (dark things absorb more and warm up), or **refracted**, bent as it passes from one material into another. The eye relies on refraction to focus.

## The outside of the eye {#outside}

::diagram{id="eye" highlight="cornea,iris,pupil" caption="The right eye seen from above; light enters on the left."}

The [[pupil]] is the dark opening that lets light in. Around it, the colored [[iris]] is a ring of muscle: it shrinks the pupil in bright light and widens it in the dark.

The clear [[cornea]] is the front window, touching the air. The [[conjunctiva]] lines the inside of the eyelids and folds back over the white of the eye; it's easily infected but protects the eye.

::focus{highlight="sclera,extraocular-muscles,optic-nerve"}

The tough white [[sclera]] forms the rest of the outer wall. Six [[extraocular muscles|extraocular-muscles]] attach to it and move the eye: four rectus muscles and two obliques. At the back, the [[optic nerve|optic-nerve]] carries signals to the brain.

::check{q="q-eye-muscles"}

## Focusing the image {#focus}

::focus{highlight="cornea,aqueous-humor,lens,ciliary-muscle,zonule-fibers"}

Light is bent first, and most, by the cornea, then passes through the clear [[aqueous humor|aqueous-humor]] and the pupil to the [[lens]]. The lens fine-tunes the focus. The ring-shaped [[ciliary muscle|ciliary-muscle]] changes the lens's shape through the [[zonule fibers|zonule-fibers]] that hold it, so you can focus near or far.

::focus{highlight="vitreous-humor,retina"}

The light then crosses the jelly-filled [[vitreous humor|vitreous-humor]] to the [[retina]], the thin sheet of neurons lining the back of the eye.

::check{q="q-eye-lens"}

## Fovea and blind spot {#fovea}

::focus{highlight="fovea,macula,optic-disk"}

The [[fovea]] is a small pit in the center of the retina, packed with cones. It gives your **sharpest** vision, the part you aim at whatever you're looking at. The [[macula]] around it is still very sharp. Few blood vessels cross this area, so they don't get in the way of the light.

Nearby, the [[optic disk|optic-disk]] is where axons leave the eye as the optic nerve. It has no photoreceptors, so it's a **blind spot**. You don't notice it because the other eye covers that part of the scene and the brain fills in the gap.

::check{q="q-eye-blindspot"}

## An upside-down map {#map}

Each point on the retina corresponds to one point in what you see, but the image is flipped both ways: the upper left of the scene lands on the lower right of the retina. For the eye, **nasal** means toward the nose and **temporal** means toward the temple.
