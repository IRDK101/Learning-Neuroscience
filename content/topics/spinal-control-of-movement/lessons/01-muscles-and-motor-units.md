## Kinds of muscle {#kinds}

- [[Smooth muscle|smooth-muscle]] lines the gut, blood vessels, airways and the iris. You don't control it consciously; the autonomic nervous system does.
- [[Striated muscle|striated-muscle]] looks striped under a microscope. Most of it is **skeletal muscle**, attached to bones and under voluntary control. The heart is **cardiac** muscle, striated but involuntary.

## Opposing pairs {#pairs}

Skeletal muscles move joints by pulling bones, and they come in opposing pairs ([[flexors and extensors|flexor-extensor]]). At the elbow, the biceps **flexes** (closes the joint) and the triceps **extends** (opens it). The muscle doing the movement is the agonist; its opponent, the antagonist, has to relax.

## Lower motor neurons {#lmn}

::diagram{id="spinal-reflex" highlight="alpha-motor-neuron,ventral-horn,ventral-root" caption="Motor neurons sit in the ventral horn."}

Each [[muscle fiber|muscle-fiber]] contracts when a motor neuron releases acetylcholine onto it at the neuromuscular junction. Those neurons are [[alpha motor neurons|alpha-motor-neuron]], also called lower motor neurons. Their cell bodies are in the [[ventral horn|ventral-horn]] of the spinal cord, and their axons leave through the [[ventral root|ventral-root]]. Like the sensory side, motor neurons are arranged by spinal segment.

::check{q="q-spin-ventral"}

## Motor units and pools {#units}

- A **[[motor unit|motor-unit]]** is one alpha motor neuron plus all the muscle fibers it contacts.
- A **[[motor neuron pool|motor-neuron-pool]]** is all the alpha motor neurons that supply one muscle.

How many fibers one neuron controls, the [[innervation ratio|innervation-ratio]], varies a lot. In the legs one neuron may drive thousands of fibers, good for force. In the fingers and the muscles that move the eyes, a neuron drives only a few, good for precision.

## Grading force {#force}

The nervous system controls force in two ways ([[grading muscle force|muscle-force]]):

1. **Firing faster.** One slow-firing neuron gives separate, jerky twitches. Fire it faster and the twitches fuse into steady tension.
2. **Recruiting more motor units** to fire together.

Motor units come in three types, and most muscles have a mix:

| Type | Force | Tires? | Used for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Slow | Low | Hardly at all | Posture (anti-gravity muscles) |
| Fast fatigue-resistant | Medium | Slowly | Walking, jogging |
| Fast fatigable | High | Quickly | Sprinting, lifting, escaping |

## Where the inputs come from {#inputs}

Alpha motor neurons get input from three sources:

- **Upper motor neurons** in the brain, the main source of voluntary commands.
- **[[Spinal interneurons|spinal-interneuron]]**, the largest input, both excitatory and inhibitory. Inhibitory ones keep opposing muscles from contracting at the same time.
- **Muscle spindles**, which report muscle length (next lesson).

## When motor neurons fail {#disease}

- **[[ALS|als]]** (amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, Lou Gehrig's disease) destroys both upper and lower motor neurons. The muscles themselves are healthy, but without their nerve supply they weaken and waste away.
- **[[Bell's palsy|bells-palsy]]** is sudden weakness of one side of the face from inflammation of the [[facial nerve|facial-nerve]] (cranial nerve VII). It's usually temporary.
