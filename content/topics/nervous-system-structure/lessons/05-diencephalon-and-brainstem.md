## The diencephalon {#diencephalon}

::diagram{id="brain-midsagittal" highlight="thalamus,hypothalamus" caption="The midline view, front on the left."}

::focus{highlight="thalamus"}

The **thalamus** is the gatekeeper of sensory information. It routes each sense to the right part of the cortex, so it comes up again in every sensory topic. Smell is the one sense that doesn't have to go through it first.

::focus{highlight="hypothalamus,pituitary-gland"}

The **hypothalamus**, just below, regulates the many body systems we don't think about, often through hormones released by the **pituitary gland** beneath it.

::check{q="q-struct-thalamus"}

## The brainstem {#brainstem}

::focus{highlight="midbrain,pons,medulla"}

The **brainstem** sits at the base of the brain, below the thalamus, and has three parts from top to bottom: the **midbrain**, the **pons** and the **medulla**. It blends smoothly into the spinal cord.

::focus{highlight="superior-colliculus,inferior-colliculus"}

The **colliculi** are two pairs of bumps forming the roof of the midbrain {{C-011}}. The superior colliculi process visual information and the inferior colliculi auditory information.

## The cerebellum {#cerebellum}

::focus{highlight="cerebellum"}

The **cerebellum**, "little brain", sits behind the brainstem, below the occipital lobe. It's small, but it contains a remarkable share of the brain's neurons (about 70–80%), and it coordinates and fine-tunes movement.

::check{q="q-struct-cerebellum"}

## The spinal cord {#spinal-cord}

::focus{highlight="spinal-cord"}

The **spinal cord** continues down from the medulla. It's divided into regions that serve different parts of the body. Below the point where the cord itself ends, the spinal nerve roots continue down as a bundle called the **cauda equina** ("horse's tail") {{C-012}}.
