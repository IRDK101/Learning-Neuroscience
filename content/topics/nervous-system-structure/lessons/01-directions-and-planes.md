:::added
**Why bother?** Every diagram and every textbook describes brain parts by where they sit relative to each other. Learn these few words once and everything else gets much easier to read.
:::

## On four legs {#quadruped}

::diagram{id="orientation" highlight="anterior,posterior" caption="Tap any arrow to see its definition."}

The terms were invented for animals on all fours, where the nervous system runs in a straight line from nose to tail.

- **Anterior** (or *rostral*, "toward the beak") means toward the front, the nose.
- **Posterior** (or *caudal*, "toward the tail") means toward the back.

::focus{highlight="dorsal,ventral"}

- **Dorsal** means toward the back or top (think *dorsal fin*).
- **Ventral** means toward the belly or bottom.

## Standing up: the 90° bend {#humans}

::focus{highlight="superior,inferior"}

Humans stand upright, so our nervous system bends about 90° where the brainstem meets the brain. In the brain, the top of the head is **superior** (also *dorsal*) and the jaw side is **inferior** (also *ventral*). Front and back are still **anterior** and **posterior**.

::focus{highlight="dorsal,ventral"}

Once you're in the brainstem and spinal cord, the axis turns downward. There, **dorsal** means toward your back and **ventral** toward your belly. Picture yourself on all fours facing the ground: whatever points up is dorsal.

::check{q="q-struct-dorsal-spinal"}

## Medial and lateral {#medial-lateral}

::focus{highlight="medial,lateral"}

Seen from above, **medial** means toward the midline that splits left from right, and **lateral** means out toward either side. You'll also meet **ipsilateral** (same side) and **contralateral** (opposite side). Many pathways cross, so the left brain often handles the right side of the body.

## Three ways to slice a brain {#planes}

::focus{highlight="coronal-plane,sagittal-plane,horizontal-plane"}

- A **coronal** slice is vertical and parallel to your face, like slicing a loaf of bread from front to back.
- A **sagittal** slice is vertical from front to back and divides left from right. The *midsagittal* cut goes straight down between the eyes, between the hemispheres.
- A **horizontal** (axial, transverse) slice is parallel to the ground, like a stack of cheese slices.
