## Two big families {#types}

- **[[Declarative memory|declarative-memory]]** (explicit) is what you can put into words. It has two kinds: **[[semantic memory|semantic-memory]]**, facts like "Paris is the capital of France", and **[[episodic memory|episodic-memory]]**, events you lived through, like your last birthday.
- **[[Nondeclarative memory|nondeclarative-memory]]** (implicit) shows up in what you do. It includes skills and habits (the striatum), conditioned body movements (the cerebellum) and conditioned emotional responses like fear (the amygdala).

## Stages {#stages}

::pathway{id="memory-stages"}

Memories pass through stages. **Sensory memory** is a brief trace in sensory cortex such as V1 or A1; only what you pay attention to goes further. **Working memory** is a mental scratchpad for what you're thinking about right now, kept alive by rehearsal, such as repeating a phone number. **Short-term memory** lasts minutes to hours and fades unless it's consolidated into **long-term memory**, which can last a lifetime.

Recalling a memory doesn't just read it out. Each recall makes the memory changeable again before it's stored anew, slightly altered ([[reconsolidation]]).

## The working memory model {#model}

A widely used model of [[working memory|working-memory]] has a **central executive** that directs attention and coordinates three helpers:

- the **phonological loop**, which keeps words alive by rehearsing them,
- the **visuospatial sketchpad**, which holds images and locations,
- the **episodic buffer**, which combines information from the other two and from long-term memory.

All three exchange information with long-term memory, so things can be pulled back into focus when needed.

## The prefrontal cortex {#pfc}

::diagram{id="brain-lateral" highlight="prefrontal-cortex,parietal-lobe" caption="Working memory relies on frontal and parietal cortex."}

Working memory depends heavily on the prefrontal cortex:

- In the [[Wisconsin Card Sorting Test|wisconsin-card-sort]], people sort cards by a rule they have to discover, and the rule changes partway through without warning. People with prefrontal damage keep using the old rule.
- In the [[delayed-response task|delayed-response-task]], a monkey sees food hidden in one of two wells, waits behind a screen, then chooses. Neurons in the [[dorsolateral prefrontal cortex|dorsolateral-prefrontal-cortex]] fire steadily throughout the wait, holding the location in mind.

The parietal cortex helps too. A monkey looking at a central point sees a target flash off to one side and has to remember where it was before moving its eyes there. Neurons in the [[lateral intraparietal area|lateral-intraparietal-area]] (LIP) stay active during the wait. The prefrontal cortex, the frontal eye fields and the LIP work together to hold locations in mind.
