## Layers of the skin {#layers}

::diagram{id="skin" highlight="epidermis,dermis,subcutaneous-layer" caption="Hairless skin on the left, hairy skin on the right."}

[[Skin|skin]] comes in two kinds: hairy skin over most of the body and [[glabrous skin|glabrous-skin]] (hairless) on the palms and soles. Both have three layers: the thin outer [[epidermis]], the thicker [[dermis]] and the fatty [[subcutaneous layer|subcutaneous-layer]].

## Five kinds of endings {#receptors}

Touch receptors are [[mechanoreceptors|mechanoreceptor]]: physical force opens **mechanically gated channels** in the nerve ending, letting Na⁺ and Ca²⁺ in. Each ending belongs to a pseudounipolar neuron whose cell body sits in a dorsal root ganglion beside the spinal cord.

::focus{highlight="meissner-corpuscle"}

- **[[Meissner corpuscles|meissner-corpuscle]]** sit in the ridges of the dermis just under the surface, and **only in hairless skin**. They sense fine touch, texture and low-frequency vibration.

::focus{highlight="merkel-cell"}

- **[[Merkel cells|merkel-cell]]** sit at the base of the epidermis in both skin types. They're the most sensitive and have the finest spatial resolution, so they're best for shape and texture, but poor at vibration.

::focus{highlight="ruffini-ending"}

- **[[Ruffini endings|ruffini-ending]]**, deep in the dermis, sense skin **stretch**. That helps you know where your fingers are relative to each other.

::focus{highlight="pacinian-corpuscle"}

- **[[Pacinian corpuscles|pacinian-corpuscle]]** are the deepest, at the border of the dermis and the fat. They sense deep pressure and high-frequency vibration, such as an object starting to slip from your grip.

::focus{highlight="free-nerve-ending"}

- **[[Free nerve endings|free-nerve-ending]]** reach up into the epidermis and signal pain and temperature.

## Receptive fields {#fields}

Each sensory neuron responds to touch on one patch of skin, its [[receptive field|receptive-field]]. Meissner and Merkel endings have tiny fields that give precise information. Pacinian and Ruffini endings have large ones, up to a whole finger or part of the palm.

## Slow and rapid adaptation {#adaptation}

Press on the skin and hold it there:

- [[Slowly adapting|slowly-adapting]] receptors (**Merkel, Ruffini**) keep firing the whole time, slowly tapering but staying above baseline.
- [[Rapidly adapting|rapidly-adapting]] receptors (**Meissner, Pacinian**) fire a burst when the touch starts and another when it ends, and are quiet in between. They care about **change**.

A Pacinian corpuscle's onion-like layers explain this. Pressing shifts the fluid between the layers and deforms the ending. Once the fluid settles, the ending is no longer squeezed, even though the pressure is still there. Removing the object shifts the fluid again.
