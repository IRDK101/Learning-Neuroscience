## Muscle spindles {#spindle}

::diagram{id="spinal-reflex" highlight="muscle-spindle,ia-axon" caption="Only one side of each circuit is drawn."}

Is the movement going to plan? [[Proprioception|proprioception]] answers that. Inside each muscle are [[muscle spindles|muscle-spindle]], thin stretch-sensing fibers wrapped by fast [[Ia axons|ia-axon]]. When the muscle stretches, the Ia axon fires.

## The stretch reflex {#stretch}

::pathway{id="stretch-reflex"}

At the doctor's, a tap just below the knee **stretches** the quadriceps. Its spindles fire, and in the spinal cord the Ia axons excite the quadriceps' own alpha motor neurons **directly**, with a single synapse. The quadriceps contracts and the leg kicks. No brain is involved, so it's fast.

The same loop helps in daily life. Pick up something heavier than you expected, and the muscle gets stretched more than planned. The spindles fire and the muscle automatically contracts harder.

::check{q="q-spin-knee"}

## Gamma motor neurons {#gamma}

::focus{highlight="gamma-motor-neuron,muscle-spindle"}

When a muscle contracts, it shortens, and an unattended spindle would go slack and stop reporting. [[Gamma motor neurons|gamma-motor-neuron]] prevent that. They fire along with the alpha motor neurons and tighten the spindle's own fibers to match, so the spindle keeps giving accurate, real-time information.

::check{q="q-spin-gamma"}

## Golgi tendon organs {#gto}

::focus{highlight="golgi-tendon-organ,ib-axon,spinal-interneuron"}

::pathway{id="golgi-tendon-reflex"}

[[Golgi tendon organs|golgi-tendon-organ]] sit where muscle joins tendon and measure **tension**. Their [[Ib axons|ib-axon]] (a little slower than Ia) excite an inhibitory interneuron in the cord, which quiets the alpha motor neurons of the same muscle. If a contraction gets dangerously strong, the muscle eases off before it can tear the tendon.

::check{q="q-spin-gto"}

## Reciprocal inhibition {#reciprocal}

::focus{highlight="spinal-interneuron,alpha-motor-neuron"}

::pathway{id="reciprocal-inhibition"}

A muscle can't work if its opponent pulls back at the same time. When Ia axons from the quadriceps excite its motor neurons, a branch also excites an **inhibitory interneuron** that silences the hamstring's motor neurons. This push–pull, [[reciprocal inhibition|reciprocal-inhibition]], is built into the cord, so the brain doesn't have to manage it.

::check{q="q-spin-reciprocal"}

## Walking without the brain {#walking}

Much of walking's rhythm comes from circuits in the spinal cord itself ([[spinal walking circuits|central-pattern-generator]]). After spinal cord injury, the brain's commands can't get through, but the circuits below the injury still work. Implanted stimulators can drive basic stepping. A **brain–spine interface** goes further: it reads movement signals from the brain wirelessly and stimulates the cord below the injury, so people can step when they intend to. The legs still need to bear some weight, and these circuits can't adapt to surprises the way the brain can.
